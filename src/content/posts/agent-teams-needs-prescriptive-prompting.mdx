The pitch for delegating to subagents is that you describe the goal and the
orchestration sorts itself out. In practice, "sorts itself out" means the model
decides — and a decision left to judgment is a decision that varies between
runs.

What I found is narrower and more useful than "it doesn't work": the routing
behaves consistently when the instruction is prescriptive, and inconsistently
when it's merely permissive. Telling it what it *may* do is not the same as
telling it what to do.

The pattern I'd draw out of it: negative constraints with an explicit trigger
condition hold up, and positive suggestions about when something is
"appropriate" don't. "Do not do X unless the user asks for it" survives; "use X
when it would be helpful" hands the judgment straight back.

## Why this is worth writing down

There's very little honest writing about agent orchestration that isn't
marketing. The available material describes the intended behavior, which is not
the same as documenting how it behaves when you're relying on it.

## Takeaways

1. **Permissive phrasing delegates the decision back to the model.** If you care
   about the outcome, phrase it as a constraint.
2. **Inconsistency is the signal, not the noise.** One good run tells you
   nothing; the variance between runs is the actual finding.
3. **Version-stamp anything you conclude here.** Routing is exactly the kind of
   behavior that shifts between releases.
